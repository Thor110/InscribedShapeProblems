Inscribed Shape Problems

Mechanical jigs, visualisations and proofs for the inscribed square, rectangle, triangle, tetrahedron and cube problems, from Jordan curves to fractal surfaces.

What has been proved

  • No cube in general: a generic smooth convex surface has no inscribed cube (Theorem 1).
  • Simplices in convex bodies: an elementary proof of regular simplices in every admissible orientation (Theorems 2–3), and why admissibility is needed (Proposition 5).
  • Squares near an ellipse, fractals included: every curve within 0.15 of a 1 × 0.6 ellipse has an inscribed square (Theorem 6), proved live in your browser in interval arithmetic.
  • Tetrahedra near an ellipsoid: every surface within 0.13 of a 1 × 0.8 × 0.6 ellipsoid has an inscribed regular tetrahedron (Theorem 7), with a range of orientations.
  • Odd perfect numbers: Euler's form, the steps of 6 and forced steps, machine-checked in Lean.
The open problems themselves (squares and tetrahedra in every continuous curve and surface, odd perfect numbers) remain open. The computer-assisted proofs are confirmed by three independent programs. Details: PROOFS.md · the preprint.

Square Certificate

A computer-assisted proof (PROOFS.md, Theorem 6) that every curve whose radius stays within T = 0.15 of a 1 × 0.6 ellipse's has an inscribed square, including nowhere-differentiable, non-symmetric fractal curves such as the one drawn here. The proof runs in your browser below, in interval arithmetic, in under a second.
Left: the curve, the ellipse (dashed), and a square found numerically on the fractal. Right: the proof. The edge of a box of candidate squares is mapped to the square equations G = (g₁, g₂) (green). It stays close to the dashed square, which is what the linearised equations would give, so it winds once around the origin and keeps well away from it. A perturbation of size T moves it by at most (1 + √2)T, the grey square, so no perturbation that small can remove the square.

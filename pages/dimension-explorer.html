Dimension Explorer

The skeletal jig in any dimension: rays from a pivot along the vertex directions of a regular polytope, solved until every ray has the same length. Each cell runs a fresh search and reports whether an inscribed copy was verified (all pairwise distances and surface distances to 1e-9) or the closest miss.

Expected dimension = dimension of similarities of ℝⁿ, n(n+1)/2 + 1, minus the number of vertices. When it is negative (amber), a generic surface has no inscribed copy; this is proved in PROOFS.md (Theorem 1), so those cells are expected to fail except on symmetric surfaces such as the sphere and ellipsoid. Hypercube rows in 5D and above have 32+ vertices and take longest.

The proved eggs, in 3D and 4D

The 3D egg of Theorem 1.3 has no inscribed cube: that is proved by computer. Its 4D extension, whose slice w = 0 is the 3D egg, is proved to be a smooth, strictly convex 3-sphere, but whether it has an inscribed tesseract is not settled. Search for the closest cube or tesseract. The solver places every vertex on the egg and then tries to make the shape exact; the colours show how far each vertex is from where a true cube or tesseract would put it (green within 0.1% of the edge, amber within 1%, red beyond). Grey dots are points of the egg; in 4D the view also turns through the fourth dimension, so the tesseract's cells pass through one another.
